# Randomized sotorasib dose comparison

The randomized comparison provides clinical context for the PK checks and the hypothetical design scenarios. In previously treated KRAS G12C-mutated advanced NSCLC, 960 mg had a numerically higher response rate and more frequent Grade 3 or higher treatment-emergent adverse events than 240 mg. The study was designed to estimate outcomes at each dose and the difference in response rates; it was not powered for formal hypothesis testing. These results do not establish dose equivalence or identify a preferred dose from one endpoint alone. [Hochmair et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.114204).

## Efficacy

Efficacy includes all randomized patients: 104 assigned to 960 mg and 105 assigned to 240 mg. The data cutoff was June 23, 2023. Response and progression were assessed by blinded independent central review. [Table 2 and Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.114204).

| Outcome | 960 mg once daily | 240 mg once daily |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed objective response | 34/104, 32.7% (95% CI 23.8–42.6) | 26/105, 24.8% (95% CI 16.9–34.1) |
| Disease control | 90/104, 86.5% (95% CI 78.5–92.4) | 86/105, 81.9% (95% CI 73.2–88.7) |
| Median progression-free survival | 5.4 months (95% CI 4.2–6.9) | 5.6 months (95% CI 4.1–8.3) |
| Median overall survival | 13.0 months (95% CI 10.7–18.8) | 11.7 months (95% CI 9.5–15.4) |

The reported stratified response-rate difference, 960 mg minus 240 mg, was **6.9 percentage points (90% CI −3.4 to 17.1)**. The unstratified difference was 7.9 points (90% CI −2.3 to 18.2). These intervals are 90% intervals, while the arm-specific response intervals above are 95% intervals. The stratified hazard ratios for 960 mg versus 240 mg were 0.95 (95% CI 0.67–1.35) for progression-free survival and 0.75 (95% CI 0.53–1.07) for overall survival. [Table 2 and Figure 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.114204).

## Safety and tolerability

Safety includes patients who received at least one dose: 104 in each arm. The data cutoff was January 18, 2023, earlier than the efficacy cutoff. These are **treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs)**; occurrence during treatment does not establish that treatment caused the event. [Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.114204).

| Patients with an event | 960 mg once daily | 240 mg once daily |
|---|---|---|
| Any TEAE | 101/104 (97.1%) | 96/104 (92.3%) |
| Grade 3 or higher TEAE | 64/104 (61.5%) | 51/104 (49.0%) |
| Serious TEAE | 38/104 (36.5%) | 34/104 (32.7%) |
| TEAE leading to interruption or reduction | 49/104 (47.1%) | 41/104 (39.4%) |
| TEAE leading to discontinuation | 17/104 (16.3%) | 13/104 (12.5%) |
| Diarrhea TEAE | 41/104 (39.4%) | 33/104 (31.7%) |

Dose reductions were permitted only in the 960-mg arm. The combined interruption-or-reduction endpoint therefore reflects different management options in the two arms. These percentages count patients with events, not the total number of events, and the rows overlap. [Sections 2.1 and 3.5, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejca.2024.114204).

## Interpretation

Randomization helps compare assigned starting doses. It does not remove uncertainty in the estimates or make the exposure groups in the separate FDA analysis comparable. The response difference allows a range of effects in either direction; a confidence interval that includes zero does not establish equivalence. Survival estimates account for censoring and cannot be reconstructed as simple event proportions from these summaries. Differences between medians should be read alongside the reported hazard ratios.

The safety results describe the observed treatment experience, including dose modifications, through their own cutoff. They are not exposure-adjusted rates and should not be combined with the later efficacy results into a single calculated benefit-risk score. FDA's later dosage guidance recommends assessing activity, safety, and tolerability across doses and explains that a randomized dose comparison need not meet the power requirements of a registrational superiority or noninferiority trial. [FDA guidance, Sections III.B–C, August 2024](https://www.fda.gov/media/164555/download).
